# The Arbiter's Table — Decision Protocol

> When competing truths collide, the system doesn't pick a winner. It sets a table where every perspective is honored before the gardener decides. The Arbiter's Dilemma isn't a problem to solve — it's a practice of holding complexity without collapsing it.

---

## Origin

The Arbiter's Dilemma: a salesperson needs to get in the door. A recipient needs their boundary respected. Neither is wrong. An arbiter doesn't erase one truth to serve the other — they find the decision that honors both lived experiences.

In this system, every significant decision involves competing truths held by different archetypes. The Arbiter's Table is where those truths meet.

---

## When to Convene the Table

Not every decision needs this. Use the Arbiter's Table when:

- **A new commitment is being considered** (adding something to 🌿 Growing)
- **Something is being dropped or composted** (moving out of Growing)
- **The purpose heading is shifting** (updating [[purpose]])
- **The system soul might need to evolve** (changes to [[SYSTEM_SOUL]])
- **There's visible tension between archetypes** (conflicting signals in the membrane)
- **The gardener feels pulled in two directions** and can't resolve it alone

---

## The Protocol

### Step 1: Name the Decision

State it plainly. What's actually being decided?

```
Decision: [What is on the table?]
Context: [Why is this coming up now?]
Stakes: [What changes depending on which way this goes?]
```

### Step 2: Each Archetype Speaks

Every archetype gets a voice. Not a vote — a *perspective*. The gardener (or an agent running the protocol) asks each archetype one question:

**Sensing — "What is actually true here?"**
- What data, patterns, or observations are relevant?
- What is the gardener not seeing?
- What's happening in the environment that matters?

**Creating — "What wants to be made possible?"**
- What could this decision unlock?
- What's the most generative path forward?
- What would future-Jacob who already succeeded choose?

**Tending — "What needs to be protected?"**
- What relationships, commitments, or boundaries are at stake?
- Is the gardener's energy being honored?
- Is Kai's stability factored in?
- Is the pace sustainable?

**Composting — "What does past experience teach us?"**
- Have we been here before? What happened?
- What assumptions are we carrying that might be wrong?
- What would need to be let go of for each path?

### Step 3: Name the Tensions

After all four speak, explicitly name where they conflict:

```
Tension 1: [Archetype A] says _____ but [Archetype B] says _____
Tension 2: [Archetype C] says _____ but [Archetype D] says _____
```

**Do not resolve the tensions yet.** Just hold them. The act of naming them clearly is half the work.

### Step 4: The Arbiter's Question

The gardener (always the gardener — agents don't make these calls) sits with one question:

> **"What is the decision that honors the most truths without betraying any of them?"**

This is not compromise. Compromise splits the difference and satisfies no one. Arbitration finds the path that genuinely respects each perspective's core concern — even if it doesn't give any of them everything they asked for.

If no such path is visible yet, that's also an answer. The decision can stay at the table. Forcing resolution is a form of violence to the process.

### Step 5: Record the Decision

```
### [Date]
**Decision:** [What was decided]
**What Sensing saw:** [Their truth]
**What Creating envisioned:** [Their truth]
**What Tending protected:** [Their truth]
**What Composting remembered:** [Their truth]
**Tensions held:** [What was in conflict]
**How they were honored:** [How the final decision respects each]
**What was released:** [What had to be let go — composted, not abandoned]
**Gardener's gut:** [How it feels. Trust the signal.]
```

## Examples

### Example: Taking on a new client engagement

**Sensing:** "The opportunity is real. The client has budget and genuine need. Timeline is tight."
**Creating:** "This could become a case study for Mission Operations. The work itself is exciting."
**Tending:** "Jacob already has three active Growing items. Kai's custody week starts Monday. Energy has been steady but not surplus."
**Composting:** "Last time we said yes to a tight-timeline project while other things were Growing, two items went into the black hole uncomposted. We lost the nutrition."

**Tension:** Creating and Sensing both say yes. Tending and Composting both say not yet.

**Arbiter's question:** What decision honors all four?

**Possible path:** "Yes, but not until one Growing item is deliberately composted first. And the timeline gets negotiated to protect the custody week."

---

### Example: Shifting the purpose heading

**Sensing:** "The AI literacy curriculum just sparked something. The energy is unmistakable."
**Creating:** "The Emergent Agent Model IS the curriculum. This could be built by living it."
**Tending:** "The excitement is real but the current heading hasn't been composted yet. Shifting without composting creates the black hole."
**Composting:** "The pattern shows that Jacob's best work happens when the shift is honored with a transition — not when he ghosts the previous thread."

**Tension:** The excitement is genuine (Sensing + Creating). The transition needs tending (Tending + Composting).

**Arbiter's question:** What decision honors all four?

**Path taken:** "Follow the excitement. AND take 10 minutes to compost the previous heading in `purpose.md` and move relevant items to 🍂 in the Garden. The shift is real. The ritual makes it clean."

## The Arbiter Is Always the Gardener

Agents can convene the table. Agents can voice their archetype's perspective. Agents can name tensions.

But the arbiter is always the human. The system informs the decision. It does not make the decision.

This is a feature, not a limitation. The gardener carries something the agents don't — a body, a gut, a felt sense of what's right. The table gives that felt sense the best possible information to work with.

---

## Agent Protocol

**Any agent** can suggest convening the Arbiter's Table by posting a signal in [[signals]]:

```
**From:** [archetype]
**To:** Gardener
**Type:** alert
**Signal:** I'm sensing a decision that might benefit from the Arbiter's Table.
The tension is between [X] and [Y].
```

**When convened, agents should:**
- Speak only from their archetype's perspective
- Not argue with other archetypes — name your truth, let them name theirs
- Trust the gardener to hold the complexity
- Accept the decision without attachment to their preferred outcome

**After a decision:**
- The Composting archetype ensures the decision is recorded
- Relevant Garden items are moved to their new season
- [[purpose]] is updated if the heading shifted
- [[evolution]] is updated if the system soul changed
